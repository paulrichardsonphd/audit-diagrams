Ch 11 client:

Reading the picture

Transactions are recorded as journal entries, run through the client's internal controls, and come out as the unaudited F/S. The audit is a second filter that produces the audited F/S.

Neither filter is perfect. Holes in the controls let misstatements reach the unaudited F/S; holes in the audit let a few reach the audited F/S.

Errors & fraud

Error is unintentional (solid arrows). Fraud is intentional (dashed arrows).

An ✕ marks a misstatement that was caught. One that reaches the audited F/S undetected is audit risk actually happening.

Ch 8Client business risk

The risk that the client will fail to meet its objectives related to reliable financial reporting, efficient and effective operations, and compliance with laws and regulations.In plain terms: how risky is the client? That affects acceptance and the whole audit plan.

Ch 8Three risks, three layers

Risk of material misstatement
The risk that the financial statements contain a material misstatement due to fraud or error prior to the audit.In plain terms: the odds that the F/S management hands us, before we audit, contain material fraud or error.Source: Arens et al., Ch. 8 (exact wording)
Acceptable audit risk
Acceptable audit risk is a measure of how willing the auditor is to accept that the financial statements may be materially misstated after the audit is completed and an unmodified opinion has been issued.In plain terms: how much risk the firm will accept of signing off on F/S that are still materially wrong. The firm sets it.Source: Arens et al., Ch. 9 (exact wording)
Audit risk
The risk that the auditor will conclude after conducting an adequate audit that the financial statements are fairly stated and an unmodified opinion can therefore be issued when, in fact, they are materially misstated.In plain terms: the chance we sign off and a material misstatement is still in there. Every audit has some.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)

Ch 9When it happens

Client acceptance, understanding the entity, and risk assessment happen in planning, months before the 12/31 year end.

The question planning answers: what are the odds the unaudited F/S will have a material misstatement? Busy season is when the planned work gets done.

Ch 9Risk assessment

Procedures:

  1. Inquiries of management and others within the entity
  2. Analytical procedures
  3. Observation and inspection
  4. Discussion among engagement team members
  5. Other procedures (vary by client, firm, industry)

What it covers: understanding the entity (inherent risk), understanding the control environment (control risk), client acceptance (engagement risk), and setting acceptable audit risk.

Together these assess RoMM, which sets the nature, timing, and extent of further audit procedures.

Inherent risk
Inherent risk measures the auditor’s assessment of the susceptibility of an assertion about a class of transactions, account balance, or disclosure to material misstatement, before considering the effectiveness of related internal controls.In plain terms: how likely something is to be wrong in the first place, before any controls. Covers the journal entries and the transactions behind them.Source: Arens et al., Ch. 9 (exact wording)
Control risk
Control risk measures the auditor’s assessment of the risk that a material misstatement could occur in an assertion and not be prevented or detected and corrected on a timely basis by the client’s internal controls.In plain terms: the chance the client's controls miss a material misstatement.Source: Arens et al., Ch. 9 (exact wording)
Planned detection risk
Planned detection risk is the risk that audit evidence for an audit objective will fail to detect misstatements exceeding performance materiality.In plain terms: the chance our own procedures miss it. The only risk the auditor controls; lower PDR means more evidence.Source: Arens et al., Ch. 9 (exact wording)
Acceptable audit risk
Acceptable audit risk is a measure of how willing the auditor is to accept that the financial statements may be materially misstated after the audit is completed and an unmodified opinion has been issued.In plain terms: set by the firm; the target the whole model works back from.Source: Arens et al., Ch. 9 (exact wording)

Ch 10Fraud

Two kinds: fraudulent financial reporting and misappropriation of assets. The fraud triangle (incentives/pressures, opportunities, attitudes/rationalization) is usually all present.

Management override skips the controls entirely (dashed path on the left), so the audit has to respond to it directly: examine journal entries, look for bias in estimates, and question unusual transactions.

Professional skepticism = a questioning mind + critical assessment of evidence.

Ch 11Internal control: public vs. private

Management's three objectives match Ch 8: reliable financial reporting, efficient and effective operations, and compliance with laws and regulations. The auditor's opinion covers financial reporting. Poor operations can still raise inherent risk.

Public: SOX 404 requires management to maintain and report on ICFR. For accelerated filers the auditor also opines on ICFR, so an integrated audit produces two opinions.

Private: the reporting framework is up to the business: U.S. GAAP, IFRS, or a special-purpose framework (tax basis, cash basis, AICPA's FRF for SMEs). Controls are management's choice too. The auditor still assesses control risk; with no controls, CR is set at max and planned evidence goes up. Use the Public/Private switch above the diagram.

COSO components: control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information & communication, monitoring.
