These are the accounts receivable and sales workpapers a first-year associate might prepare. Every number ties, and a few things go wrong on purpose.

Every workpaperThe parts of a workpaper

  • Heading: client, title, period, and the W/P reference that files it in the binder.
  • Sign-offs: who prepared it, who reviewed it, and when.
  • Objective and procedure: what the auditor was trying to learn, and exactly what was done, including where the sample came from.
  • Tickmarks and legend: a shorthand for each step performed on each item.
  • Findings and conclusion: what was found, how it was resolved, and what the auditor concluded.

The PCAOB requires documentation clear enough that an experienced auditor with no connection to the engagement could tell what was done, what evidence was obtained, what was concluded, and who did and reviewed the work (AS 1215, Audit Documentation).
