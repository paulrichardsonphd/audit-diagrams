Set materiality for the F/S as a whole

Planning

Pick one benchmark and apply a rule-of-thumb percentage. This is the overall (preliminary) materiality: how "wrong" the F/S as a whole can be.

Key terms
Materiality
The magnitude of an omission or misstatement of accounting information in the financial statements that individually or in the aggregate is substantially likely to influence the judgment made by a reasonable user of the financial statements.In plain terms: an error or omission is material if it’s big enough to change a reasonable user’s decision.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)
Materiality: the legal standard
In interpreting the federal securities laws, the Supreme Court of the United States has held that a fact is material if there is “a substantial likelihood that the . . . fact would have been viewed by the reasonable investor as having significantly altered the ‘total mix’ of information made available.”In plain terms: the same idea from the Supreme Court, which the PCAOB uses: would a reasonable investor see it differently?Source: PCAOB AS 2105.02 (exact wording)
Preliminary judgment about materiality (overall materiality)
The maximum amount by which the auditor believes that the statements could be misstated and still not affect the decisions of reasonable users; used in audit planning.In plain terms: the most the F/S as a whole can be off before users would care. Set during planning; it’s the overall materiality in this step.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)

Check the numbers yourself: every AMD amount on this page comes from AMD's Form 10-K for fiscal 2025 (year ended December 27, 2025), Item 8: the consolidated statement of operations and consolidated balance sheet. The line it comes from is listed under each amount.

Allocate overall materiality to the accounts: how much each account can be off (its performance materiality). Each account's amount is set below overall materiality to leave a cushion, because small misstatements in several accounts, including ones the audit doesn't catch, can add up.

Key terms
Allocation of the preliminary judgment about materiality
The process of assigning to each balance sheet account the misstatement amount considered to be material for that account based on the auditor’s preliminary judgment.In plain terms: splitting overall materiality across the accounts: how much each account can be off. That’s the table below.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)
Performance materiality
The materiality amount(s) for segments of the audit, set by the auditor at less than materiality for the financial statements as a whole.In plain terms: a lower amount for each part of the audit, leaving a cushion so small errors in different accounts, including ones the audit misses, are unlikely to add up past overall materiality.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)
Tolerable misstatement
The application of performance materiality to a sampling procedure (AICPA standards) or the materiality allocated to any given account balance (PCAOB standards).In plain terms: two meanings. Under the AICPA (and on this page), it’s performance materiality applied to one test, like each bank account below. Under the PCAOB, it’s the amount allocated to an account, which this page calls performance materiality.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)

Key terms
Known misstatements
Specific misstatements in a class of transactions or account balance identified during the audit.In plain terms: errors we can measure exactly, like the books vs. the bank for cash.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)
Likely misstatements
Misstatements that arise either from differences between management’s and the auditor’s judgment about estimates of account balances or from projections of misstatements based on the auditor’s test of a sample from a population.In plain terms: errors we estimate rather than measure: a projection from a sample (AR below) or a disagreement with management about an estimate.Source: Arens et al., glossary (exact wording)

Keep straight

Materiality is relativeA $1M error matters for a small company and not for AMD. That's why it's a % of a benchmark.
Quantitative and qualitativeA small misstatement can still be material (fraud, illegal acts, flipping a loss to income).
Known vs. likelyKnown: exact amount (GL vs. bank). Likely: projected from a sample (AR).
Performance materiality is inversely related to evidenceLower performance materiality → more evidence needed.
