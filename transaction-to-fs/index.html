Reading the picture

A business event (selling a computer on account) runs through the client's process, gets recorded as a journal entry, and is posted to the ledger and summarized into the financial statements. The audit opinion is over management's F/S.

Ch 6Management's assertions

By issuing F/S, management implicitly asserts that the numbers are right. Auditors have objectives over both the entries and the balances.

Existence or occurrence
Assets or liabilities of the company exist at a given date, and recorded transactions have occurred during a given period.In plain terms: it's real and it happened.
Completeness
All transactions and accounts that should be presented in the financial statements are so included.In plain terms: nothing is missing.
Valuation or allocation
Asset, liability, equity, revenue, and expense components have been included in the financial statements at appropriate amounts.In plain terms: the amounts are right, in the right period and account.
Rights and obligations
The company holds or controls rights to the assets, and liabilities are obligations of the company at a given date.In plain terms: the company owns it or owes it.
Presentation and disclosure
The components of the financial statements are properly classified, described, and disclosed.In plain terms: shown and explained clearly.

Source: PCAOB AS 1105.11 (exact wording)

Ch 14 · 16Direction matters

Trace forward from source documents (order, shipping doc) to the journal: did every sale get recorded? That tests completeness.

Vouch backward from the journal to the documents: did this recorded sale really happen? That tests occurrence.

Ch 6 · 13Transaction-related objectives

Occurrence
The transactions in the journals actually took place.In plain terms: did it really happen?
Completeness
No transaction that took place was left out of the journals, and no required disclosure is missing.In plain terms: did everything that happened get recorded?
Accuracy
Each recorded transaction carries the right dollar amount (correct quantity, price, and math), and the related disclosures get the numbers right.In plain terms: right dollar amount?
Posting & summarization
Transactions flow correctly from the journals into the subsidiary records and the general ledger, and the totals add up.In plain terms: carried correctly into the subledgers and totals?
Classification
Each transaction is recorded in the correct account.In plain terms: right account?
Timing
Each transaction is recorded in the period, and on the date, it took place.In plain terms: right date?
Presentation
Transactions are combined or broken out in a sensible way and described clearly, and the related disclosures help a reader follow them.In plain terms: grouped and described so a reader understands it?

Author’s definitions; the PCAOB defines the assertions these support (AS 1105.11).

Ch 6 · 16Balance-related objectives

Existence
The assets and liabilities on the balance sheet are really there at the balance sheet date.In plain terms: is it really there?
Completeness
Every asset and liability that exists is included in the balance, along with the disclosures that go with it.In plain terms: is everything included?
Accuracy
Each item in the balance is recorded at the right dollar amount, and the related disclosures get the numbers right.In plain terms: right amount?
Classification
Each item in the balance sits in the right account.In plain terms: right account?
Cutoff
Transactions near year end land in the right period, not pushed into next year or pulled into this one.In plain terms: right period?
Detail tie-in
The detail behind the balance (for example, the list of customer balances) adds up correctly and agrees with the general ledger.In plain terms: do the details add up to the general ledger?
Realizable value
Assets are reported at no more than the company expects to actually collect or recover.In plain terms: will we actually collect it? (e.g., AR net of allowance)
Rights & obligations
The company owns or controls the assets it reports, and the liabilities it reports are its own.In plain terms: does the company own it or owe it?
Presentation
Balances are combined or broken out in a sensible way and labeled clearly, and the related disclosures help a reader follow them.In plain terms: shown and disclosed clearly?

Author’s definitions; the PCAOB defines the assertions these support (AS 1105.11).

Ch 13Where each test points

1 ToC tests the client's process (e.g., credit approval before shipping). 2 STOT tests individual entries against the transaction-related objectives. 3 SAP runs analytics on the balances (e.g., AR or sales vs. last year) to flag where to look. 4 TDB tests the ending balance (e.g., confirm AR).

1 and 2 cover the 7 transaction-related objectives; 3 and 4 cover the 9 balance-related objectives.

Good results from 1 and 2 let the auditor do less of 4.

Ch 14Finding a known misstatement

Testing accuracy, the auditor inspects the sales invoice. The JE says $1,500; the invoice says $1,000. That's a $500 known misstatement.

Compare known and likely misstatements to performance materiality (e.g., sales materiality of $10,000) to decide whether more work is needed.
